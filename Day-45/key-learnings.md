# Key Learnings — Day 45

## 1. Structured Decision-Making
I learned that a difficult decision becomes easier to evaluate when it is broken into clear parts instead of being treated as one emotional question.

## 2. Decision Matrix
A decision matrix can turn vague preferences into comparable scores. The seven dimensions used here are:
- Life/Career Upside
- Financial Safety
- Growth & Learning
- Stress Level
- Reversibility
- Long-term Alignment
- Regret Risk

## 3. Cognitive Biases
I learned that biases such as loss aversion and status quo bias can influence decisions without being obvious. Naming the bias makes it easier to challenge the reasoning.

## 4. Premortem Thinking
A premortem asks what could cause a decision to fail before committing to it. This helps identify early warning signs and prevention actions.

## 5. Reversibility Matters
Not every decision is permanent. Considering how easy a decision is to undo can reduce unnecessary fear and improve risk assessment.

## 6. Experiment Before Committing
The 7-day test plan showed me how small experiments and conversations can produce useful evidence before making a larger commitment.

## 7. UI/UX Learning
I practiced creating:
- Card-based layouts
- CSS variables
- Animated progress bars
- Responsive mobile layouts
- Visual winner states
- Compact screenshot-friendly cards

## 8. Claude AI Learning
This project demonstrated how a carefully designed prompt can turn a conversation into a structured final artifact. Clear rules, interview questions, content requirements, and design specifications help produce a more consistent application.

## 9. Important Principle
The goal of a decision framework is not to create certainty. It is to make assumptions, trade-offs, risks, and priorities visible enough to support a better decision.

## 10. Day 45 Takeaway
**Good decision-making is less about finding a risk-free choice and more about making the trade-offs clear, testing important assumptions, and knowing what evidence would change the decision.**
