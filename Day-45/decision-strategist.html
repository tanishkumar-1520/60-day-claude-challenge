3 — Assumption Buster

3 assumptions you may be making
  • You may be treating an uncertain outcome as more predictable than it really is.
  • You may be assuming that avoiding change automatically reduces risk.
  • You may be assuming the decision is permanent when parts of it may be reversible.
2 biases at play
  • Loss aversion: potential losses can feel heavier than equivalent gains.
  • Status quo bias: the current path can feel safer simply because it is familiar.
1 thing you may be ignoring

Your stated goal should matter more than the discomfort created by uncertainty.

5 — Premortem

Option A — If it failed after 12 months

  • The underlying assumption proved wrong.
  • The expected upside did not materialize.
  • The cost or stress became larger than expected.

Early warning: progress or evidence consistently moves away from the original goal.

Prevention: define a measurable checkpoint before committing fully.

Option B — If it failed after 12 months

  • The option did not deliver the expected outcome.
  • The trade-off became harder to tolerate.
  • A better alternative appeared but was ignored.

Early warning: repeated signals contradict the original reasoning.

Prevention: schedule a deliberate review point and define an exit condition.

6 — 7-Day Test Plan

Day 1: Research the highest-impact unknown.
Day 2: Verify the most important assumption with evidence.
Day 3: Run one small, low-risk experiment for Option A.
Day 4: Run one small, low-risk experiment for Option B.
Day 5: Have one honest conversation with someone who can challenge your thinking.
Day 6: Ask what evidence would make you change your mind.
Day 7: Decide using the goal, evidence, reversibility, and pre-defined criteria—not fear alone.
