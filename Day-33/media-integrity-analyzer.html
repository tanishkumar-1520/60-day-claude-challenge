Challenge 01 · Headline Detective

Can you separate the claim from the spin?

Read the headline and article together. Your job is not to know the “right answer” — it is to notice what the wording makes you assume.

Before you begin

Headlines are compressed stories.

A headline has very little space, so writers choose words carefully. Words such as “shocking,” “everyone,” “proves,” or “secret” can make a claim sound stronger than the evidence. This matters whenever you encounter news, search results, notifications, or viral posts.

Challenge 02 · Emotion Detector

Notice what the content makes you feel.

Emotional reactions can be useful signals. They can also be deliberately engineered to make us stop, share, buy, or agree.

Before you begin

Emotion is part of the message.

Media can use fear, outrage, pride, urgency, belonging, or nostalgia to influence attention. This does not automatically make a message false. It means you should notice the emotional layer before deciding what to believe or do.

Three practical habits

  • 1Check the claim. Compare the strongest words in a headline with the actual evidence in the article.
  • 2Pause on strong emotions. Fear and anger can increase attention before accuracy has been established.
  • 3Ask “who is this for?” Notice who benefits, who is being targeted, and what action the content wants.
