# Day 29 – Operation Lifeline: Supply Chain Crisis Lab

## Project Overview

Today I built **Operation Lifeline: Supply Chain Crisis Lab**, an interactive supply chain crisis management simulator using React, HTML, CSS, and JavaScript.

The application simulates realistic business disruptions and allows the player to make strategic decisions as a supply chain leader.

## Objective

The main objective was to understand how organizations respond to unexpected supply chain disruptions while balancing:

* Cost
* Inventory
* Profit
* Delivery Speed
* Customer Satisfaction
* Supplier Relationships
* Business Resilience
* Risk Management

## Features Implemented

### 1. Welcome Screen

* Project title and description
* Executive decision simulator introduction
* Start Simulation button

### 2. Random Company Generation

Each simulation generates a fictional company with different:

* Industry
* Revenue
* Number of factories
* Warehouses
* Suppliers
* Inventory days
* Global sourcing countries

### 3. Crisis Generation

The simulator randomly generates supply chain crises such as:

* Factory Fire
* Supplier Bankruptcy
* Port Strike
* Cyberattack
* Flood
* Raw Material Shortage
* Political Conflict
* Shipping Delay

Each crisis includes its urgency level and potential business impact.

### 4. War Room

The player receives six possible crisis-response actions and must select three.

The selected actions affect business metrics such as:

* Cost
* Inventory
* Profit
* Delivery Speed
* Customer Satisfaction
* Resilience

Animated progress bars are used to visualize the changing business condition.

### 5. Supplier Negotiation

The simulator includes four supplier negotiation rounds.

Each decision changes:

* Supplier Trust
* Price
* Lead Time

The negotiation section demonstrates how aggressive pricing, long-term partnerships, premium capacity, and information sharing can produce different outcomes.

### 6. CEO Boardroom

The player answers five leadership questions covering:

* Customer continuity
* Emergency spending
* Supplier decisions
* Executive KPIs
* Post-crisis resilience

The answers contribute to the final leadership score.

### 7. AI Strategy

The player selects two AI investments from:

* Demand Forecasting
* Inventory Optimization
* Supplier Risk Monitoring
* Warehouse Vision
* Procurement Copilot

Each investment has different expected business impacts.

### 8. Executive Dashboard

The final dashboard displays:

* Overall Crisis Score
* Leadership
* Negotiation
* Resilience
* Cost Control
* Risk Management
* Customer Satisfaction

It also provides:

* Best Decision
* Biggest Mistake
* Expert Recommendation
* Lessons Learned

### 9. Replay System

The simulation can be replayed to generate different fictional companies, crises, and decision scenarios.

## Technologies Used

* HTML5
* CSS3
* JavaScript
* React 18
* Babel JSX
* React `useState`
* Responsive CSS
* Browser-based simulation logic

## Design Approach

The application uses a premium enterprise-dashboard style with:

* Dark theme
* Rounded cards
* Responsive layouts
* Progress bars
* Hover effects
* Smooth transitions
* Mobile-friendly design
* Clear decision explanations

## Key Outcome

This project demonstrates how an interactive business simulation can be used to understand supply chain decision-making. Instead of simply presenting information, the simulator allows users to experience the consequences of different operational and strategic decisions.

## Conclusion

Day 29 helped me understand the connection between **supply chain resilience, crisis response, supplier negotiation, executive leadership, and AI-driven decision-making**.

The biggest takeaway is that effective crisis management is not about optimizing a single metric. Leaders must balance cost, customer commitments, inventory, speed, risk, and long-term resilience simultaneously.
